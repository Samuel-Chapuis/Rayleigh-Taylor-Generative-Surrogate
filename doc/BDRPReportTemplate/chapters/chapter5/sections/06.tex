\section{Wavelet-based results and comparison with baseline diffusion}

The wavelet cascade is assessed in two distinct settings. In the
\emph{reference-coarse} diagnostic, the $cA^2$ coefficient is taken from a
validation field and only the detail sub-bands are synthesized. This is not an
unconditional generation experiment: it isolates the quality of the
conditional detail models. In the \emph{generated-coarse} setting, the
initial $cA^2$ is itself sampled by a diffusion model, so that errors can
propagate through the full cascade.

Figure~\ref{fig:ch5_phyfid_steps} makes the separation between the two WSGM
settings quantitative. With a reference coarse coefficient, PhyFID is
$3.6\times10^{-3}$ at $64$ reverse steps and remains below $10^{-1}$ down to
$8$ steps. The conditional detail process can therefore be sampled with far
fewer steps than the direct SGM in this controlled setting. In contrast, the
unconditional cascade is near $2.83$ at $64$ steps and changes little as the
number of steps is increased; its limiting factor is the sampled coarse
coefficient, not the numerical integration accuracy of the detail stages.

The reference-coarse result nevertheless establishes an important target for
the method: the conditional detail models are not the primary bottleneck. If
the coarse diffusion stage is improved so that its distribution is compatible
with the conditions encountered during detail-model training, the WSGM should
be able to approach the direct SGM quality while requiring substantially fewer
reverse steps in the detail generation. This remains a hypothesis supported by
the controlled ablation, not a demonstrated end-to-end acceleration.
