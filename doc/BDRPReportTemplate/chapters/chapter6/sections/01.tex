\section{Limitations}

The main limitations of this work are the small size of the DNS dataset, the
partial quantitative evaluation of the wavelet-based model, and the current
roughness of reconstructed wavelet-generated fields. The diffusion architecture
and conditioning strategy were initially designed close to image-space
generation and may not be optimal for the statistical structure of wavelet
coefficients.

The WSGM results must also be interpreted with care. The fully generated
cascade is faster to sample than the direct image-space SGM, because the
generation is distributed across lower-dimensional wavelet coefficients.
However, this acceleration is presently obtained at the cost of lower sample
quality: the generated fields remain rougher, contain more visible artifacts,
and match the validation distribution less accurately than the baseline. The
current WSGM should therefore be seen as a faster but less accurate alternative,
not as a model that already dominates the direct SGM.

Finally, the reference-coarse experiment is not a useful generative setting in
itself, since it uses coarse information taken from validation data. Its role is
diagnostic: it isolates the conditional detail stages and shows that they can
produce accurate fine-scale content when the coarse coefficient is already
physically consistent. The limiting factor of the present WSGM pipeline is
therefore the unconditional coarse diffusion stage.
